# Website & App Mockups: The Library (M01–M15)

A mockup is **proof**. The screens *are* the product, so they must be real, readable and exact.
The scene around them must look like a real product photograph finished by a Photoshop expert,
with a wow. The rules the AI follows are in [AI.md §15](../AI.md); this page is the template
library it picks from when you don't bring your own.

> M13–M15 were added from clean, real Dribbble shots: notice how sharp and exact their screens are — that
> is the bar for every recipe.
>
> These are **written scene recipes**, not image files. The look of each one is described precisely
> enough that an image model can render it in HD with your real screens. Stock mockup previews are
> usually low-res, watermarked or AI-generated, so none are copied into this repo.

---

## How to use a recipe
1. Pick a recipe (or let the AI suggest 2–3 for your product).
2. Capture the screens it needs (see **Screens** in each recipe, and the capture guide below).
3. The AI writes the WOW concept, the layer plan and the prompt, and picks a route:
   **one-step** (AI renders screens too) or **two-step** (blank screens + you place the real
   screenshots in Photopea with its placement recipe).

## Capture guide (do this well and everything else gets easy)
- Browser window at **1440×900** (or 1920×1080), zoom 100%, **2× scale**, no bookmarks bar, no extensions.
- Full-page screenshots (for scroll mockups): 1440 wide, whole page.
- Phone: **390×844** or your real phone; clean status bar (9:41, full battery, full signal).
- Tablet: 1180×820 landscape or 820×1180 portrait.
- **Demo accounts with realistic sample data.** Never real client names, emails or money.
- Same page and brand state across devices when the story is "responsive".

## The rules every recipe follows
- Screens are **smart objects**: exact UI, correct perspective and corner radius, sharp pixels.
- Screens are the **brightest, sharpest** thing in the frame (white ≈ 92–95%, never blown out);
  environment one stop darker; reflections subtle and never over text.
- The main screen fills **≥ 35%** of the frame; text-heavy screens face the camera within ~20°.
- Generic, **unbranded devices**; real materials; one light direction; contact shadows.
- 1–3 **signature moves** (the wow), everything else quiet.

## The library

### M01 — Device Family Trio (front view)
**Best for:** responsive websites, landing pages · **Screens:** desktop + tablet + phone
**Scene:** laptop centre-back, tablet right overlapping its corner, phone front-left overlapping
both; eye-level camera, 50mm, near-straight-on. Seamless light-grey or brand-tint sweep.
**Light & world:** soft key from upper left, soft floor shadows; one large brand-colour circle or arc
shape behind the devices (flat, matte).
**Wow:** the overlapping family + the brand shape cutting behind them.
**Text slot:** optional logo top-left and a one-line caption bottom-centre.
**Route:** two-step for text-heavy pages.

### M02 — Hand-Held Phone in a Real Interior (POV)
**Best for:** mobile apps, consumer services · **Screens:** one phone screen
**Scene:** a hand holding a generic phone toward the camera, phone fills 55–65% of the height,
straight-on; behind it a softly blurred real room that matches the app's world (living room for home
services, café for food, office for B2B).
**Light & world:** soft window light from the side; natural skin on the hand, correct grip, five
fingers; screen bright and sharp.
**Wow:** shallow depth of field — razor-sharp screen against a creamy real interior.
**Route:** one-step works (one big screen); check every word.

### M03 — Unrolling Website (creative)
**Best for:** long landing pages, agency portfolios · **Screens:** one full-page screenshot
**Scene:** an open laptop at a slight 3/4 angle; the website's full page flows out of the screen and
down over the keyboard edge like a printed sheet, gently curling toward the viewer.
**Light & world:** white or soft-grey studio wall with vertical slat texture, a soft leaf shadow from
a window; the paper curl has real shading.
**Wow:** the page literally coming out of the screen.
**Route:** two-step strongly recommended (curved surfaces garble text): render the scene with a
blank page, then warp the full-page screenshot onto it (Edit → Transform → Warp).

### M04 — Desk Setup, Four Devices
**Best for:** responsive websites, "we build for every screen" · **Screens:** monitor, laptop, tablet, phone
**Scene:** a clean desk: large monitor centre, laptop right, tablet and phone standing left, keyboard
in front, one plant and a mug as props; camera at desk height, slight downward angle.
**Light & world:** soft daylight from a window on the left; warm-neutral room, matte desk.
**Wow:** the same page perfectly consistent across four sizes.
**Route:** two-step (four screens).

### M05 — Floating Page Grid
**Best for:** multi-page websites, templates, case studies · **Screens:** 4–6 page screenshots + 1 laptop
**Scene:** a laptop centre-bottom; around and above it, 4–6 browser windows floating in a grid at
slightly different depths, each numbered 01–06 in large light numerals.
**Light & world:** one-colour studio (sage, sand, pale blue), soft shadows under each window.
**Wow:** the whole site at a glance, editorial numbering.
**Route:** two-step.

### M06 — Dashboard + Phone with Studio Props
**Best for:** SaaS with web dashboard + mobile app · **Screens:** desktop dashboard + phone app
**Scene:** a large monitor/dashboard angled back-left, a phone in the front-right tilted toward the
viewer, both on a studio set with matte podiums and a few spheres in brand colour and white.
**Light & world:** soft top light, gentle reflections on a satin floor; brand colour only in props.
**Wow:** two products, one system — with sculptural studio props.
**Route:** two-step for the dashboard; phone can be one-step if the text is big.

### M07 — Tablet Flat-Lay on Colour
**Best for:** dashboards, wellness/finance apps, portfolios · **Screens:** one tablet landscape
**Scene:** a tablet straight-on, centred, filling 75% of the width, on a soft single-colour backdrop
taken from the product's palette; a soft drop shadow.
**Light & world:** soft even top light; no props, or one small object at the edge.
**Wow:** calm, gallery-like precision; the UI is the hero.
**Route:** one-step often works; check text.

### M08 — Phone Trio on a Surface
**Best for:** mobile apps with several key screens · **Screens:** 3 phone screens
**Scene:** three phones standing upright side by side on a stone/plaster ledge, the middle one slightly
forward; plants at the edges; window-light leaf shadows across the wall.
**Light & world:** warm afternoon light from the right, real shadows of the phones on the wall.
**Wow:** natural light and shadows make it feel photographed, not rendered.
**Route:** two-step (three screens).

### M09 — Editorial Number Showcase
**Best for:** portfolios, Behance/Dribbble case studies · **Screens:** 1–2 page screenshots
**Scene:** one large page tilted slightly in perspective on a warm flat colour; a giant light numeral
("01") behind it; an elegant serif title top-left ("Website Showcase") with a small caption line.
**Light & world:** flat studio colour, soft shadows; refined, magazine-like.
**Wow:** editorial typography + giant numerals.
**Route:** two-step (tilted page).

### M10 — Tall Page on Laptop (isolated)
**Best for:** long landing pages · **Screens:** one full-page screenshot
**Scene:** a laptop seen from slightly above; a tall portrait sheet of the full page stands upright out
of the screen area, much taller than the laptop; solid pastel backdrop; small script caption top.
**Light & world:** soft frontal light, soft floor shadow.
**Wow:** the whole page visible in one tall, clean object.
**Route:** two-step.

### M11 — Browser + Phone Flat Pair
**Best for:** simple responsive sites, launches · **Screens:** one desktop page + one phone screen
**Scene:** a flat browser window on the left (70% width) and a phone standing on the right overlapping
its edge, straight-on, on a single warm colour; tiny caption line above.
**Light & world:** flat even light, soft shadows; minimal.
**Wow:** perfect alignment and colour harmony between backdrop and site.
**Route:** one-step can work for big hero screens; two-step for detail.

### M12 — Person + Devices (lifestyle showcase)
**Best for:** e-commerce, fashion, personal brands, agencies · **Screens:** monitor/laptop + phone
**Scene:** a full-body person standing beside or leaning toward a large monitor and a phone showcase,
on a seamless light-grey studio sweep; the person's styling matches the brand (and, for a shop, the
product being sold).
**Light & world:** soft key from front-left, matched light on person and devices, real floor shadows.
**Person:** the user's real photo (§8.B) or a photographic generated model (§8.C).
**Wow:** a real human scale next to the product; brand world in clothes and colours.
**Route:** two-step for screens; person can be generated with the scene.

### M13 — Browser on Wallpaper (Dribbble shot)
**Best for:** landing pages, SaaS hero sections, Dribbble/Behance · **Screens:** one desktop page (1440 wide)
**Scene:** a clean, generic browser window (traffic-light dots, sidebar and back/forward icons, a
centred address bar with the site's **real URL**, share/new-tab icons) straight-on, centred, about
83% of the width, its bottom edge cropped slightly by the frame or sitting just above it. Behind it, an
abstract wallpaper of large, soft, flowing colour waves built from the product's palette (e.g.
magenta → violet → indigo), like a desktop background.
**Light & world:** the browser casts a soft, wide shadow on the wallpaper; the page is crisp and
evenly lit; the wallpaper is out of focus slightly at the edges.
**Wow:** a vivid, ownable colour world framing a calm, perfectly sharp UI.
**Route:** two-step or code composite (flat browser, exact page) — the wallpaper can be generated.
Use an original wave wallpaper, never a copy of an operating-system wallpaper.

### M14 — Laptop Front-On, Dark Gradient
**Best for:** product launches, hero images, ads · **Screens:** one desktop page
**Scene:** a generic modern laptop, perfectly front-on at eye level, the screen filling about 74% of the
width; thin black bezels, a small webcam, slim aluminium base visible at the bottom; **no brand logo**.
Background: a dark, rich gradient in the brand's deep colour (e.g. midnight purple) with one or two
very soft diagonal light sweeps.
**Light & world:** soft top light; a subtle highlight along the base edge; the screen is the brightest
thing in the frame; faint reflection of the screen glow on the base.
**Wow:** cinematic contrast — the bright page glowing out of a dark world.
**Route:** two-step (blank screen, then place the page with Distort) or one-step only if the hero text is big.

### M15 — Macro Close-Up (cropped browser)
**Best for:** showing detail and craft: typography, buttons, dashboards · **Screens:** one desktop page at 2× or 3×
**Scene:** the same generic browser window, scaled up so it runs off the right and bottom edges of
the frame, starting about 6% from the left and 8% from the top; we see the address bar, the nav and
the hero large and razor-sharp. A sliver of the colourful wallpaper shows at the left and top edges,
softly blurred (shallow depth of field).
**Light & world:** soft window shadow on the wallpaper; the page perfectly sharp; the blur only on the
wallpaper.
**Wow:** "look how clean this is up close" — detail as the hero. Pairs with M13 as a two-shot set.
**Route:** two-step or code composite with a 2×/3× screenshot (it must be sharp at this size).

## The scale rule
A screen shown at **less than ~50% of its captured width** gets retyped by image models: names,
URLs and numbers come back wrong. Those screens are always two-step (blank screens in the scene,
real screenshots placed by hand or by an AI that can run code). Proof beats prettiness: a mockup with
wrong star counts is worse than no mockup.

## Two-step placement recipe (Photopea / Photoshop)
For each screen in the generated scene:
1. Place the screenshot as a **Smart Object** above the scene.
2. **Edit → Transform → Distort**: drag the four corners to the four screen corners
   (curved pages: **Warp**; complex angles: **Perspective Warp**).
3. Add a mask with the screen's **rounded corners**; tuck it 1px inside the bezel.
4. Match brightness: Curves clipped to the screen; the screen should be the brightest thing in the
   frame, not pure white.
5. Glare: new layer, white→transparent diagonal gradient at 5–8%, blend **Screen**, clipped.
6. Bezel depth: Inner Shadow 1–2px, 20% black on the screen layer.
7. Grain: Filter → Noise 1% on a top layer so screens and scene share one "camera".
8. Check at 50% size: can you read the headline on every screen?
